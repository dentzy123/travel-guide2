## Food Tips for Travelers

Trying local food is often the highlight of travel—here are practical tips to eat well, safely, and respectfully.

### Eat like a local
- Seek busy restaurants and market stalls with high turnover.
- Ask hotel staff or locals for trusted recommendations.

### Food safety
- Prefer foods cooked thoroughly and served hot.
- Avoid raw seafood, unpasteurized dairy, and uncooked salads in high-risk areas.

### Street food
- Choose vendors with a line — turnover usually means fresher food.
- Observe how food is handled and whether ingredients are stored at safe temperatures.

### Water & beverages
- Avoid tap water in many countries; use bottled or properly filtered water.
- Avoid ice made from tap water; choose sealed drinks when unsure.

### Dietary needs & allergies
- Carry a translated allergy card and any necessary medication.
- Learn key local phrases for allergies and dietary restrictions.

### Budget-friendly tips
- Eat where locals eat: markets, food courts, and lunch specials.
- Share dishes or order small plates to taste more without overspending.

### Etiquette & tipping
- Research local customs: communal dishes, utensil use, and table manners vary.
- Tipping norms differ widely—check local guidance before paying.

### Ordering & communication
- Learn simple phrases for ordering and asking about ingredients.
- Use translation apps or printed cards to communicate dietary needs.

### Alcohol & safety
- Know local laws and cultural attitudes toward alcohol.
- Avoid unregulated spirits; choose reputable brands when possible.

### Quick checklist
- Wash or sanitize hands before eating.
- Check that food is freshly prepared and served hot.
- Keep small cash for markets and snacks.
- Have a plan for allergies or stomach issues (medication, emergency contacts).

Enjoy exploring local flavors—stay curious and stay safe. Adapt these tips to your destination and personal needs.
